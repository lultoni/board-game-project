# Playtest 5 - Elias (P1) vs Jonathan (P2) - Stack L (Pole B per-turn-draft prototype)

*Digital prototype, during 2026-06 vacation window. No exported game log - Jonathan refreshed the browser and the state was lost. **Persistence is now a tooling requirement, not a nice-to-have.***

## Result

- Jonathan (P2) won after **15 rounds**.
- First Guard death: ~R7.
- First Champion death: ~R9-R10.

## Insights (verbatim from designer, post-game)

### Armor still feels mandatory
Even in Pole B, the feeling that "you have to take Armor or you die" came back. Jonathan said unprompted that **Armor 3 is too much** - converging with all Pole-A Armor-volume data (OQ-11). Armor-as-tax is not dissolved by per-turn-draft; the structural cause is deeper than the draft model.

### Play collapsed to pure reaction
Because picks could be played immediately and the board state shifted dramatically every turn, neither player planned - both reacted. No multi-turn intent. Connects to Principle 4 (cleverness > attrition): a draft model that makes long-horizon planning impossible breaks the core fantasy.

### Combinatorial breadth broke "perfect information"
So many possible picks × actions × interactions per turn that the *feeling* of perfect information was lost, even though formally PI held. **PI in the rules ≠ PI in the experience.** This is a new datapoint distinct from OQ-62 (which is about draft determinism in Pole A).

### Trade made
Pole B replaced Pole A's "no idea what the skills do at start" problem with "too much happens per turn to plan." Not strictly better - different failure mode.

### Game went fast (which is good) but felt empty (which is bad)
15 rounds is much shorter than P4 (28-29). Short is good - but "short because you didn't think" is not what we want. The goal is **short + with overarching tactic**, not short + reactive.

## Direction shift (designer call)

Return focus to **Pole A** with two concrete sub-goals:

1. **Onboard new players better.** Solve OQ-56 Problem A (draft entry complexity) by offering **pre-made loadouts for inexperienced players** rather than asking them to draft.
2. **Drastically shorten the game.** Target **30-60 minutes** (down from ~2h30 in P4).

Pole B is not dead, but is no longer the active track. Pole B's "skills-during-play" goal (OQ-56 Problem B - mid-game adaptability) still needs a different solution path; one option is **accepting non-adaptability if games are short enough** that a bad loadout doesn't dominate an hour of play.

## Combo bonus widening
Increasing the counter for combo bonus is wanted - but the **"single piece hits multiple Champs"** variant of the widened combo bonus felt **too much** in play (or at least staged in user's head as too generous). Narrow the widening proposal accordingly when Stack A G3 is revisited.

## Tooling consequence
**The digital prototype needs persistence.** Browser refresh wiping state cost us a real game log + feedback export. This is now blocking - any future Pole B (or other digital) playtest must persist state.
