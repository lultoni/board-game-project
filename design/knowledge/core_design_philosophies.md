# Core design philosophies

This doc shows all the core design principles and decision that have been made that will not be compromised on when thinking of new ideas and improvements for the game.

The list:
1. **Keep it simple and easy to understand/pick up.** "That means the game state itself creates interesting decisions. Rather than making every unit intrinsically complex, aim for simple rules that produce changing value through interaction. Designers often call this emergent complexity: the depth comes from how mechanics combine, not from long lists of exceptions." Or also: "A small number of interlocking systems that generate surprising, meaningful decisions."
2. The Game will be a square-grid-based tactical 1v1 board game.
3. Perfect Information in actual play, so no dice or randomness that a player can blame. Randomness or hidden information that affect both players the same in any situation is fine, but not the preffered route to take.
4. **Reduce things to keep track of.** So no temporary effects or timers or hidden cards or even terrain effects. This point is about reducing the amount of mental math that players have to make during a single turn. If it is too complex for no reason or there are too many possibilites then the players won't actually be able to plan for both their own turn and their oppoents response (like they should be able to). In the same way that game length itself is a form of attrition - when players have to think too much to play decently they will not be mentally fit for the most interesing parts of the game later on. If a single full player turn has too many different options then they do not feel like they have perfect information anymore even tho they formally have perfect information.
5. The true goal of the game is to have fun and to find clever solutions to the puzzle. The game hence should not become a single "i have to lock in and calculate 10 moves in advance to win against my opponent", but rather a "hey i wanna play this and see what the opponent can come up with". but i still do not want an infinitely broad search tree.
6. Champions are blank slates that get their identity from the skills they have equiped, not by being pre-named. We want players to have the mental freedom to do anything they like.
7. Every Strategy Archetype should have a moment where its the best on the board. No single strategy should dominate above any other one. Hence players should not be forced to play a certain way "just to not lose".
8. Don't reward symptoms, reward the system (The KPI Principle). Rewarding "dealing damage" with bonus Money incentivises the symptom (hitting things) rather than the system (clever play). Like company KPIs: setting wrong targets leads to good-on-paper results with a worse product. Reward the entire cycle, not one metric. This should make it so that when we propose changes or look at feedback we asses them through the lense of the entire game fantasy lens and not just that isolated rule for example.
9. Cleverness = multi-turn positional setup rewarded with a payoff that exceeds what grinding (just running in with your pieces) achieves. If a 3-turn setup and a stumble-into-position produce the same result, the game doesn't reward cleverness.
10. While the core game idea is not final we should perfer fundamental shits over tweaking variables as to not get stuck in a deep end instead of just making a holistic change.
11. The player should always want to do more than they are actually able to (both in form of having more money to use skills or the actions for either movement or skills themselves)
