## Key Findings

1. **Layer 1 economy fix works — skills from Round 1.** Both players used skills immediately. The dead opening is eliminated. Skill Slots, not Runes, became the primary action limiter in the early game (Elias: "die Skill Slots haben die Bremse"). This is the intended design.

2. **Game ended at Round 26 — session ran 4+ hours.** R26 was the final round played (Draw). Elias's post-game note estimates ~10 more rounds would have been needed for a win condition — a natural finish at ~R36. The session took 4+ hours despite only 26 rounds, pointing to long think times as a major contributor alongside round count.

3. **First (and only) Champion kill came at Round 26 — the final round.** "Unexpected target tho." Only one piece kill in the entire game. Pieces are extremely durable at 2HP with armor stacking. Layer 2 (3HP) risks making kills even rarer — this is the most important watch item for Layer 2.

4. **Positional deadlock replaced dead opening.** At R14 Elias wrote "stalling — no clear attack angle; two guards in front of each other like pawns." The Rune-starvation dead opening is fixed, but a new bottleneck appeared: positional gridlock where guards block attack lanes. Importantly, Elias also wrote "very nice!" — suggesting this felt like tense tactical manoeuvring, not frustrating downtime. A qualitatively different problem from P1.

5. **Skill combos generating named positive moments.** At R12 Elias wrote "peak move!" after landing a Quick Dash + Focus Strike + Lance Thrust injury chain. At R13 he wrote "gradients" after the first piece entered Injured state. At R15–17 he noted "a lot of thinking and together." These are signs the core combo fantasy is working.

6. **Think times remain long — caused by combo depth, not confusion.** At R22 Elias explicitly flagged "there are still very long think times." Importantly, the cause is that the combo system gives players so many options they always want to find the best move. This is a double-edged finding: the depth is working as intended, but it's a direct contributor to the 4-hour session length. The solution is likely a shorter board/fewer pieces, not simplifying the combo system.

7. **Injured state became relevant.** Both players rated it "Often." Elias: "injured pieces could still threaten with Focus." Elias's log shows "gradients" noted at first injury. Jonathan's emphatic "Yes." Both used heal/armor skills. Major improvement over Playtest 1.

8. **Bodyguard data is uncertain.** Jonathan wrote "?" on his feedback form — he was unsure whether it triggered. Game log shows he used Guard-adjacent positioning tactically, but no clean confirmation. Treat Bodyguard improvement as tentative pending Layer 3.

9. **Rune Theft is a soft flag, not an urgent problem.** Elias: "a bit — aber für jetzt okay — note for later." Jonathan used it 3+ times but didn't flag it. Deferred — monitor in Layer 2, don't act on it yet.

10. **Multiple rules ambiguities surfaced mid-game.** At least 6 rulings needed. Must be codified before Layer 2.

11. **Elias explicitly validated the core design direction.** Game length note: "mit besserer Strat maybe kürzer, aber der Core stimmt eig, es müsste nur runterskaliert werden i think." The combo system and turn structure are right — the fix is scaling down (board size, piece count), not redesigning the core.

12. **Overall enjoyment dramatically higher than Playtest 1.** Jonathan: "Mid to late game Bombe 6 out of 5." Elias: "best board game ever." Final log note: "das denken, strategisieren, kombinieren war sehr gut!" The experience is landing even if the length isn't.

## Implications for Design

### Accept Layer 1
The economy fix is a clear improvement. Skills active from Turn 1, both players rate the experience significantly better than Playtest 1. **Layer 1: ACCEPT.**

### Proceed to Layer 2 (3 HP)
The Injured state is already improving (both players: "Often"), but it's working because more 1-DMG skills are being fired — not because HP is higher. Layer 2 (3 HP for Champions/King) should deepen this further and is the natural next test.

### Rule Clarifications Required Before Next Playtest
The following ambiguities must be resolved and written into all future rule sheets:
1. **Move Slots per piece**: Each piece may only be moved **once per turn** during the Movement Phase.
2. **Default Skill Range**: Confirmed as **Range 2** (from caster tile, not counting caster tile).
3. **Range numbering**: self = Range 0, adjacent = Range 1, "range-2" = 2 tiles along skill path. Focus Strike (+1 Range) can extend Range 0 to Range 1, Range 1 to Range 2, etc. Injured (Range −1) still permits Range 0 (self).
4. **Focus Strike / Blade Call scope**: Applies to **any skill on any piece** used that same turn — not limited to the casting piece.
5. **Retreat Plan**: End tile must be adjacent to a Guard. The Guard does **not** need to be on the Skill Path.
6. **Movement-via-skills damage**: Confirmed **no damage** (Quick Dash, Shadow Shift, Retreat Plan do not deal damage on movement).
7. **Blade Tempest**: Does **not** move the caster. Only adjacent pieces around the target are pushed.
8. **Same piece, double activation**: A Champion **can** be activated with both Skill Slots in the same turn. This must be stated explicitly (Jonathan found it counter-intuitive).

### Skill Balance Flags
- **Rune Theft**: Potentially too strong with faster economy — the tempo swing from stealing Runes scales with Rune abundance. Watch in Layer 2; consider raising cost to 4 if it dominates.
- **Air Blast / Precision Thrust**: Potentially underused as offensive tools. Suggestion (Elias): allow targeting own pieces for repositioning versatility. Worth testing.

### Design Ideas to Evaluate
- **Skill pool draft**: Draft a pool first, then assign to Champions — could reduce "accidental" skill synergies and increase strategic intent. (New OQ)
- **Flexible piece placement**: Move away from fixed row placement toward player-chosen setup within constraints. (Existing OQ-9 may need reopening)
- **Smaller board**: Jonathan suggests smaller board for faster action. Aligns with Layer 5 hypothesis. Doesn't block Layer 2–4.
- **Co-op variant**: "Against the game" mode — noted as a genuinely interesting idea from the session atmosphere. Out of scope for competitive design but worth tracking.
